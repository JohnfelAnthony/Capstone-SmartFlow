# Site B photo geometry review — November 2, 2026

The owner confirmed that all four supplied Street View screenshots show National Highway × Sobrecarey/Dao beside Jollibee and CitiHardware. The imagery labels indicate June and July 2026. These are visual references, not a measured survey.

## Visible features to represent

- A raised, planted central median divides opposing highway traffic.
- Raised palm-lined side islands separate main carriageways from frontage/service lanes.
- Islands have rounded noses and terminate at openings near the intersection.
- Zebra markings are visible near island ends.
- Frontage traffic and access areas must not be represented as unrestricted extra main-road width.

## Current implementation mismatch

`data/networks/tagum_site_b.json` explicitly omits service roads and models the median crossing as one movement. `tools/build_study_network.py` produces the provisional site. The renderer draws native lane polygons, so adding trees alone would leave the engine free to travel through the apparent barriers.

The existing overlap reproduction at 53.4 seconds involves `road5f` and `road2f`. Both roads are named Davao-Agusan National Highway in the current JSON; earlier descriptions calling `road2f` the side street were incorrect. Trace their actual shapes and directions before attributing the collision to Sobrecarey Street.

## Implementation acceptance

1. Retain the current network as historical evidence. Update the generator and generated network together; record all estimated dimensions as provisional.
2. Represent opposite highway carriageways with physical separation. Side islands and frontage lanes need their own geometry, not decorative overlays on active lanes.
3. Keep junction openings free of planting; establish connected vehicle paths around island noses. Do not infer legal turns or frontage direction solely from a vehicle's orientation in a still image.
4. Preserve the single selected RL junction contract unless the verified topology requires a deliberate redesign. Do not silently add uncontrolled extra junctions.
5. Expose island geometry to Canvas 2D, render raised kerbs and palms from that geometry, and ensure no vehicle body intersects it.
6. Recheck all existing site cases for overlaps, conservation, release, throughput and determinism. Remove the expected-failure marker only after the zero-overlap regression passes.
7. A changed network fingerprint invalidates earlier site validation and matched baselines. Preserve old recordings with their embedded networks; do not rewrite them.

## Evidence still needed

An overhead view covering the intersection and the first island openings on all highway legs is needed to establish the connectivity that the perspective screenshots do not show completely. Main-carriageway, median, side-island and frontage widths may initially be explicitly estimated, but require measurement before field validation. Frontage travel directions and permitted crossing/turning movements need confirmation.

## Experimental connected graph and spacing constraint

`tools/site_b_frontage.py` now creates a **separate unvalidated graph** with one-way frontage segments on both sides and four provisional no-signal side-street connections. It uses the estimated 14.55 m frontage-center offset, cached side-street shapes, and estimated 200/150 m end extensions. Its new turn movements are assumptions; islands and vehicle avoidance are not implemented. `docs/reports/site-b-frontage-graph-unvalidated.json` is not a runnable or installable network.

The accompanying `docs/reports/site-b-frontage-spacing-report.json` records why: the four inner side-road segments measure approximately 14.55, 18.20, 16.25 and 25.48 m in this proposal, while the current native clipping rules require at least 26 m for each. `RoadNetwork` rejects the first short segment. Increasing these distances solely to make the parser accept them would change the proposed site layout; reducing clearances without handling interactions across nearby junctions could introduce unsafe traffic behavior. The next engine design must explicitly address nearby junction geometry, vehicle footprints and release/deadlock behavior, then receive the coordinated version/compatibility update and full acceptance checks. These are computed against provisional geometry, not field measurements.

`tools/close_junction_analysis.py` identifies two candidate groups of three physical junctions based on the current clearance and empty-link storage rules; its reproducible output is `docs/reports/site-b-close-junction-analysis.json`. These are design candidates, not certified conflict areas: other geometric conflicts, pedestrian paths, stop-line setbacks and queued vehicles also matter. A synthetic engine test that temporarily reduces the clearance leaves a bus unable to enter a 14.55 m physical link even with green at both junctions. This demonstrates that changing clipping alone does not provide a workable model. No engine behavior, version, recording or research baseline has been changed by this analysis.

The current expanded corridor was checked for 60 simulated seconds under synthetic medium no-signal demand for seeds 101 and 102; the preserved report is `docs/reports/site-b-expanded-smoke-2026-11-02.json`. Seed 102 has one overlapping pair at 45.4 s: two motorcycles on the same curved `road6r` approach before the neighboring junction. Seed 101 had no overlap in this short check. Both same-seed 60 s fingerprints repeated and conservation error was zero. Neither run completed a trip in this short window (22 and 17 unfinished), and the active network has no island polygons, so island intrusion is reported as unavailable. This is not long-duration acceptance or field validation; the seed-102 regression is a strict expected failure pending an engine fix and coordinated contract review.

A one-step synthetic reproduction isolates the seed-102 pair: both motorcycles are on `road6r`, but their headings differ across a bend; the same-lane lateral-band check does not classify the leading motorcycle as an obstacle. A test-only world-footprint guard prevented that first overlap, but two 120 s trial runs still found different-lane overlaps at 108.5 s (seed 101) and 89.6 s (seed 102). The seed-101 different-lane overlap also occurs without the prototype. The guard was not installed: same-lane checks alone are insufficient, and any eventual engine change needs cross-lane conflict, liveness, full suite, and version/recording/model compatibility checks.

The later test-only `tools/site_b_guard_prototype.py` adds world-space checks, pre-commit approach-path checks and a sideways-motion guard. It is not connected to the application. In 120 s trials both seeds had zero overlapping pairs, zero stuck junction vehicles and zero conservation error; 11/6 trips completed. In 300 s trials both had zero observed overlapping pairs and conservation error, but seed 101 reached one stuck junction vehicle (38 completed, 40 unfinished); seed 102 had none (48 completed, 34 unfinished). Trial runtimes were 104.5 s and 78.9 s respectively. The prototype therefore **fails** the stated zero-stuck criterion and needs optimization as well as a liveness fix. Island checks remain unavailable because this network contains no islands. Its geometry samples are experimental and are not proof of continuous clearance.

At 174.5 s in the seed-101 prototype, a connector vehicle was held behind two cars on opposing `road2f` and `road3f` lanes. Their present bodies did not overlap, but a small forward step by either would overlap the other, leaving both held by the guard. `tools/site_b_conflict_regions.py` and `docs/reports/site-b-conflict-regions-provisional.json` identify conservative lane/body envelope ranges that cover those positions. For this pair, the conservative range spans essentially the entire 153 m link: the long source segments and full-lane/body margins make it too broad to use as a traffic reservation. A practical correction must establish a tighter, safe conflict area and avoid admitting vehicles into an unrecoverable opposing wait; simply reserving the full range could severely reduce throughput.

`tools/site_b_oriented_regions.py` refines the review using direction-aligned envelopes and 4 m cells (`docs/reports/site-b-oriented-conflict-regions-provisional.json`); the same pair still spans the full link. Matching positions along their opposing paths are approximately 8.6 m apart near the middle even though each modelled carriageway is 9.9 m wide. As an **uninstalled experiment**, `tools/site_b_centreline_proposal.py` shifts each of the six one-way highway segments 2 m to its own right, preserving shared graph endpoints; the results and limitations are in `site-b-separated-centrelines-unvalidated.json` and `site-b-separated-centrelines-report.json`, with a schematic `site-b-separated-centrelines-overview.svg`. The opposite highway midpoint separation becomes approximately 12.6 m. This shift and endpoint transition are provisional estimates, not measured geometry, and the experiment does not add frontage or islands.

With the unchanged native engine, synthetic medium no-signal 120 s checks still showed at least one overlap for each seed; with the unregistered experimental guard, both had zero observed overlaps, stuck vehicles and conservation errors, completing 10/7 trips with 27/25 unfinished. These are short exploratory results on a different network fingerprint; neither the geometry nor the guard has passed acceptance or field validation, and no active network was replaced.

The separate 300 s guard trial is saved in `docs/reports/site-b-separated-guard-300s-provisional.json`: zero observed overlaps for both seeds, but a maximum of two stuck junction vehicles for seed 101 and one for seed 102. The runs completed 45/54 trips and left 33/28 unfinished, with zero conservation error; no islands were present. In seed 101 the first stuck pair appeared at 224 s, on `road1f` and `road2f` around the CitiHardware junction. Separating the highway centre lines therefore does not solve the side-road conflict or justify installing the prototype. The experiment runner refuses to overwrite a report and records the network fingerprint.

As a further isolated experiment, a 20 m minimum junction clip was applied in the test process only, together with the separated-centreline candidate and guard. Medium no-signal 300 s trials showed zero observed overlaps, stuck junction vehicles, gridlock and conservation errors for both seeds, with 52/44 completed and 26/38 unfinished. Medium fixed and high no-signal 120 s trials also showed zero observed overlaps and stuck vehicles; high no-signal left 54/59 unfinished. Reports are `site-b-separated-clearance20-300s-provisional.json`, `site-b-separated-clearance20-fixed-120s-provisional.json` and `site-b-separated-clearance20-high-120s-provisional.json`. This override changes the parser-generated lane geometry without changing the network JSON fingerprint, and is explicitly recorded as such; it is not the installed engine. It also makes the already-too-short proposed frontage links less feasible (two 20 m clips plus the 8 m lane minimum would require at least 48 m). Therefore the result neither solves the requested frontage geometry nor supports installation or field-validity claims.

`tools/compound_junction_prototype.py` explores a separate reservation primitive for the four short frontage links. It maps a directed raw-graph route to candidate close-junction groups and their first exits, and models atomic acquisition of all junctions in a group plus an exit resource. Tests verify that blocked checks or competing leases acquire nothing and that release requires an explicit rear-clear indication. This code is not used by the engine: its signal, pedestrian, path and downstream-storage readiness inputs are placeholders that must be established by a future implementation. Atomic acquisition alone does not prove fairness, continuous clearance, or freedom from deadlocks involving other resources.

`tools/site_b_combined_review.py` now combines the separated-centreline and frontage graph proposals without changing the registered network. The current review files are `docs/reports/site-b-combined-v2-unvalidated.json`, `site-b-combined-v2-review.json` and `site-b-combined-v2-overview.svg`. The schematic identifies the study and neighboring junctions, highway and one-way frontage directions. It does not show or enforce islands. The native parser still rejects the short frontage links; it must not be used as a simulation or treated as accepted field geometry. Twenty-seven focused tests passed and the labelled SVG was opened and visually inspected in a browser.

**Source changed during this work:** another set of uncommitted changes replaced the active Site B network with a newer image-based layout and retained the former corridor at `data/networks/history/tagum_site_b_osm_corridor.json`. The proposals and simulation results above refer to the historical corridor, not automatically to the new active layout. `tools/site_b_island_proposal.py` now produces an explicitly historical-corridor review with a 15.25 m frontage offset and nine nominal 2 m island strips; `docs/reports/site-b-island-v3-review.json` reports three strips potentially touching road pavement. The `v3` SVG marks these in red. It is not runnable or accepted. The earlier `site-b-island-v1-*` experiment was inadvertently produced from the newer layout before this source change was noticed, replacing its islands in that *separate review draft*; do not use it. The experimental centreline builder now refuses input that already contains islands or site features. No existing source geometry was overwritten by these review experiments.
